  • Robert J. Gooding-Williams on W.E.B. Du Bois' "Abolition Democracy" [0:28-0:38] (2020)

  • Ruha Benjamin: Race After Technology: Abolitionist Tools for the New Jim Code [ex. Preface, Introduction] (2019)

Discussion & Activities

  • According to Critical Resistance, what does it mean to abolish the prison-industrial complex?
  • What is the purpose of the Ubuntu centre in Jones and melo’s second story about Shanel?
  • Reflection: What aspects of Shanel’s experience with the CS department in the first story, if any, resonated with you? Why?
  • Conjuring (sticky notes): What might an abolitionist reimagining of CS- and technology-related institutions around you look like? Feel free to draw from Jones and melo’s ideas, but try to consider your specific local contexts and experiences.
  • According to Bettina L. Love, what is the difference between being an ally and being a co-conspirator?
  • According to Du Bois, what ruined our chance of having a “universal” democracy after the Civil War? How does Angela Davis pick up this thread with respect to the PIC in Abolition Democracy?
  • What does it mean that "the major systems of oppression are interlocking"? Discuss illustrative examples from the Combahee River Collective statement, what you have learned, current events, and/or the conditions of your lives (to the extent you are comfortable).
  • Think: Journal/reflect on the way(s) in which you occupy space in the margins. What perspective do you gain by existing in these margins? How might your experience in the margins give you strength, or inform mechanisms of resistance to systems of oppression?
  • Pair: Find a partner and discuss your answers to the above.
  • Share: Discuss significant insights about the above more widely.
  • Explain why Benjamin says that “the road to inequity is paved with technical fixes."
  • How does Benjamin feel about multicultural marketing and Black celebrity endorsements of tech?
  • What does it mean to view “race as technology”?
  • Why, for whom, and to what ends to we pursue computer science or technology education and production?
  • Abolition Reflection Activity

Discussion & Activities

  • Where and how do you learn (or have you learned) about the history of technology?
  • What does Dunbar-Ortiz mean when she says that “Multiculturalism became the cutting edge of...US history revisionism”?
  • Describe some technologies that are discussed in An Indigenous Peoples' History of the US.
  • What type of decision-making process did Indigenous nations or city-states described in Chapter 1 of An Indigenous Peoples' History employ? How did it work?
  • Dunbar-Ortiz writes that “The institutions of colonialism and methods for relocation, deportation, and expropriation of land had already been practiced, if not perfected, by the end of the fifteenth century.” How, why, and against whom?
  • Discuss relationships between the technological histories covered. What similarities, differences, and patterns/systemic factors do you notice?
  • What does James Baldwin mean when he says "The great force of history comes from the fact that we carry it within us, are unconsciously controlled by it in many ways, and history is literally present in all that we do”? How does this apply to the history of technology?

Surveillance & e-Carceration

Media

  • James Kilgore: Understanding e-Carceration: Electronic Monitoring, the Surveillance State, and the Future of Mass Incarceration [ex. Ch. 2, 6, 9] (2022)
  • Simone Browne: Dark Matters: On the Surveillance of Blackness (2015) [ex. Introduction]
  • Lu et al.: "Shifting from Surveillance-as-Safety to Safety-through-Noticing: A Photovoice Study with Eastside Detroit Residents" (2023)
  • Brendan Rascius: "ICE is Using Border Patrol Mobile Facial Recognition Technology to Speed Up Arrests" (2026)

Discussion & Activities

  • Anti-Surveillance: What makes you feel “seen” (in a good way)?
  • Discuss two examples from “Understanding e-Carceration” illustrating how states use e-carceration technologies to create and enforce boundaries.
  • What is the difference between physical and e-carceration in terms of the potential for individual v. collective punishment?
  • James Kilgore wrote “Understanding e-Carceration” in 2022. What has changed since then?
  • What surveillance systems exist in our own communities (for example in your neighborhood or in public buildings or institutional or corporate campuses) that perpetuate e-carceral systems? How do you interact with these systems?
  • How do we, as people who might create and maintain powerful technologies, understand systems of power? How does that understanding inform our priorities, threat modeling, and design choices? How might we work toward building power for communities?
  • Fanon observed that closed-circuit television (CCTV) surveillance was trained “not only on the potential thief, but also on ___________” (need not be a direct quote). Discuss.
  • What does Browne argue is missing from surveillance studies that her work provides?
  • What is “sousveillance”? What is “dark sousveillance”?

Algorithmic Oppression

Media

  • Ruha Benjamin: Race After Technology: Abolitionist Tools for the New Jim Code [Ch. 1-3] (2019)
  • Ruha Benjamin: Viral Justice: How We Grow the World We Want [ex. Ch. 4] (2022)
  • Os Keyes: "Automating Autism: Disability, Discourse, and Artificial Intelligence (2020)
  • Williams et al.: What Do You Mean by 'Hood' Prom?: Joyful Narratives Shown Through the Eyes of Black Teens on TikTok" (2026)
  • Safiya Umoja Noble: Algorithms of Oppression: How Search Engines Reinforce Racism (2018)
  • Virginia Eubanks: Automating Inequality: How High-Tech Tools Profile, Police, and Punish the Poor (2018)

Discussion & Activities

  • Describe a time you have felt negatively toward an algorithm on a personal level, if one exists.
  • Ruha Benajamin mentions that “Humans are tool makers and robots are humanities finest handiwork.” When we think about robots entering the mainstream either through the use of physical robots and or LLM chatbots what does the “uncanny valley” mean and what is an example you see in your daily life in respect to AI?
  • Define Racism without Racists and give one example of it.
  • Benjamin writes that “power is, if anything relational. If someone is experiencing the underside of an unjust system, others, then, are experiencing its upside” (p. 65). As technology producers and maintainers, we are (or will be) experiencing the “upside” of technological systems in the form of a paycheck and more. How do we leverage this privilege and build power toward resisting systems of technological oppression, while navigating the fact that employment deprivation is one of the “modes of suppression" of liberation movements? (Boykoff 2007)
  • Describe a time you have felt “exposed” by technology.
  • Explain what Foucault meant by “visibility is a trap,” and how this applies to technology.
  • Define a personalization algorithm and discuss some of the harms of personalization algorithms.
  • Benjamin describes the “doublebind” of simultaneous hypervisibility and invisibility: “Some technologies fail to see Blackness, while others render Black people hypervisible and expose them to systems of racial surveillance.” What are some other examples of this “doublebind” on axes besides race (such as gender, ethnicity, ability, sexual orientation, religion, etc.)?
  • Discuss the impact of Big Tech “disruption” on peoples’ lives and livelihoods.
  • Benjamin argues that less friction for some is more friction for others, challenging the assumption that tech makes life easier (whose life?). Where might you experience such friction tradeoffs in your day-to-day life?
  • Techno-Complexes Discussion

Decolonial Computing, Indigenous Knowledge Systems, & Sustainability

Media

  • Linda Tuhiwai Smith: Decolonizing Methodologies: Research and Indigenous Peoples" (1999)
  • Winona LaDuke: "Seeds of Our Ancestors: Seeds of Life" (2012)
  • Lyla June: "3000-Year-Old Solutions to Modern Problems" (2022)
  • Siddharth Kara: "Blood Batteries: The human rights and environmental impacts of cobalt mining in the Democratic Republic of the Congo" (2025)
  • Kohei Saito: Slow Down: The Degrowth Manifesto [ex. Ch. 2, 7] (2020)
  • LaToya Strong and Atasi Das ft. Judy Dow: "Abolition Science Praxis Part 2" on Abolition Science Radio (2018)

Discussion & Activities

  • Describe the land (environment) where you’re from. "Where you're from" can be interpreted however you want (ex. your homeplace, birthplace, where you grew up, a formative place, etc.)
  • Choose two of the indigenous projects discussed by in Decolonizing Methodologies and identify: a) What the project is about, and b) An example of what the project might look like in practice.
  • Linda Tuhiwai Smith mentions Graham Smith’s four models for “culturally appropriate research”: tiaki, whangai, power sharing, and empowering outcomes. Briefly explain two of these. In general, what do these models and the other models discussed in the chapter have in common?
  • How might the degrowth mindset be applied to your relationship with technology? To society?
  • Discuss Saito's critiques in the context of AI expansion and data centers.

  • Daniel Boyarin: The No-State Solution: A Jewish Manifesto [ex. Preface, Introduction] (2023)
  • Rashid Khalidi: The Hundred Years' War on Palestine: A History of Settler Colonialism and Resistance, 1917-2017 (2020)
  • Omer Bartov: "I'm a Genocide Scholar. I Know It When I See It." (2025)
  • Amnesty International: “Automated Apartheid: How Facial Recognition Fragments, Segregates and Controls Palestinians in the Occupied Palestinian Territories" (2023)
  • Antony Loewenstein: The Palestine Laboratory: How Israel Exports the Technology of Occupation Around the WorldDownload The Palestine Laboratory: How Israel Exports the Technology of Occupation Around the World [ex. Introduction, Ch. 1] (2023)

Discussion & Activities

  • Read Wikipedia sources’ discussion of Sumūd. How does this idea of Sumūd manifest in your life? How does it apply to abolition?
  • Why is the acknowledgement of erasure very important to the ways we think about colonialism? Give an example from Khalidi’s “The Hundred Years’ War on Palestine.”
  • What were some key elements of Herzl’s early Zionist strategy?
  • Drawing on example(s) from either reading, what do you think is meant by the phrase “the weaponization of anti-Semitism”?
  • What was Bartov’s critique of Holocaust scholars?
  • Where did the initial funding for Israel’s weapons research and development sector come from?
  • What does Loewenstein mean by the “The Palestine Laboratory”?
  • Comment on Israel’s involvement with the ruling powers of any other nation mentioned in Chapter 1 of The Palestine Laboratory.
  • What happened with Elbit’s tenancy at 130 Bishop Allen Drive and with its participation in MIT’s Industrial Liaison Program? What might this strategy look like for a change you want to make in your environment?

Queer Computing & Design Justice

Media

  • Sasha Costanza-Chock: “Design Justice: Towards an Intersectional Feminist Framework for Design Theory and Practice" (2018)
  • Mia Mingus: "Access Intimacy" (2022)
  • Os Keyes: The Misgendering Machines: Trans/HCI Implications of Automatic Gender Recognition" (2018)
  • Spiel, Keyes, and Barlas: "Patching Gender: Non-Binary Utopias in HCI" (2019)
  • Dean Spade: Normal Life: Administrative Violence, Critical Trans Politics, and the Limits of Law [ex. Preface, Introduction] (2009)
  • Buolamwini and Gebru: “Gender shades: Intersectional accuracy disparities in commercial gender classification" (2018)

Discussion & Activities

  • What is one of the design recommendations Keyes mentions in their work on Automatic Gender Recognition (AGR)? Describe the recommendation and the motivation behind it.
  • Give an example of an inaccuracy mentioned in Buolamwini and Gebru’s Gender Shades paper and connect it to at least one other related algorithmic inaccuracy that we have covered.
  • What role does scientific quantification of machines’ reinforcement of systemic oppression (racism, sexism, etc.) play in the practice of abolition? Why?
  • What structures of harm, if any, do they reproduce?
  • What role does raising awareness play in abolition? In reform? What’s the difference?
  • What’s the difference between demographic labeling (eg. race) and phenotypic labeling (eg. skin color), and why do Buolamwini and Gebru opt for phenotypic?
  • Why does Costanza-Chock argue that participation in design should be “fair and meaningful”? What might that look like in practice?
  • What are some ways people can actively create or support access intimacy in their relationships romantically or platonically and in their own communities?

Discussion & Activities

  • What is a “non-reformist reform”? Give an example of a “key question” you could ask in order to help determine if a reform is non-reformist.
  • What was the organization Stop LAPD Spying’s response to the POST Act of New York, which requires the NYPD to provide a “use policy” for each surveillance technology?
  • What is the difference between abolition feminism and carceral feminism? Give an example.
  • Describe the mission and work of the “Our Data Bodies” project.
  • In your own words, what is mutual aid, and how is it different from charity?
  • In what ways does Spade argue the nonprofit sector benefits rich people more than poor people?
  • How does Spade imagine “scaling up” mutual aid?
  • What is co-optation? Give an example of co-optation and how mutual aid movements can avoid it.
  • Discuss two of Spade’s elements of good meeting facilitation.
  • Explain the meaning of the Mutual Aid Disaster Relief’s slogan “No Masters, No Flakes.”
